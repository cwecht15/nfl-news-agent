Analysis & What to Watch

## Biggest Storylines

- The clearest theme of draft eve is uncertainty at the top of the board: the Raiders are widely presumed to be taking Indiana QB Fernando Mendoza at No. 1, but the draft is being framed as starting at No. 2 because no one knows what the Jets will do. The Cardinals, Titans, Giants, Browns, and Rams are all still tied into the top-10 trade/leaning conversation, which is exactly the kind of setup that can create one surprise pick or one domino-trade early in Round 1. [2, 14, 16]

- Several teams appear to be treating the draft less like a single-pick exercise and more like an asset-accumulation opportunity. Seahawks GM John Schneider said he would like to trade back from No. 32 because Seattle enters the draft with only four picks, and that fits a broader build-by-volume posture for a team that already has seen pieces from the Russell Wilson trade become part of a Super Bowl-active roster. The Raiders are also being mocked with a Day 2 trade-down, while the Packers enter without a first-round pick and may need to be aggressive if they want to move up. [18, 6, 8, 24]

- The Browns are the clearest example of an organizational reset being reflected in player comments: Dillon Gabriel said to “continue to get better,” Shedeur Sanders described a “new vibe” and “new energy” under Todd Monken, and Monken emphasized effort as the easiest thing to control. That does not settle any depth-chart questions, but it does show Cleveland trying to establish a tone and communication base before the rookie class is even finalized. [38, 50, 51, 53]

- The Rams, Steelers, and Colts are all carrying notable non-draft variables into the week. Sean McVay said Puka Nacua is a full participant in the offseason program and “looks great,” which removes one major offseason concern for Los Angeles; Pittsburgh is still waiting on Aaron Rodgers without a deadline; and Colts QB Daniel Jones has resumed dropping back and throwing again as he works back from an Achilles injury. [12, 33, 41, 47, 40]
