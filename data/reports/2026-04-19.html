## Biggest Storylines

- The Dexter Lawrence trade is the day’s defining league-wide move. The Giants sent Lawrence to the Bengals for the No. 10 pick, and Cincinnati then finalized a one-year, $28 million extension for him after the physical, turning the deal into a full win-now commitment on defense. ESPN research says it is the first time in the common draft era that the Bengals have traded a top-10 pick for a player, which underlines how aggressively they value Lawrence’s impact. [34, 28, 39]

- For the Giants, this reads as a premium-capital reset rather than a short-term talent loss. Jeremy Fowler noted they are now in position to control the first half of the draft, and the broader draft coverage suggests a board with a wide-open top 10 and heavy positional-value debate. The downside is obvious: replacing a player of Lawrence’s caliber is not simple, and the post-trade roster hole is still not specified as solved. [29, 25, 43, 13]

- Houston made the other major statement of the day by extending Will Anderson Jr. for three years and $150 million, making him the highest-paid non-quarterback. That tells you the Texans are treating Anderson as a foundational roster pillar, not just a good pass rusher, and are willing to pay early to lock in elite defensive talent. [21]

- The draft class conversation is shifting from rankings to maneuvering. Multiple reports and mock drafts point to an active top end of Round 1, with teams such as the Browns, Chiefs, Patriots, Jets, and Colts all framed around value, trade flexibility, or pick accumulation rather than strict need-based drafting. [13, 32, 40, 42, 45, 14]
